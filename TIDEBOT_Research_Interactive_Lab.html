01 · The Problem

Plastic waste reaches the water. T.I.D.E. BOT is designed to help collect it.

The research focuses on floating solid waste in the shoreline waters of Pachoca, Calapan City. The project explores whether an affordable, student-developed robotic platform can support shoreline cleanup efforts.

01

Local relevance

Pachoca was selected because of recurring floating plastic waste and the direct community relevance of testing a waste-collection prototype in the area.

02

Engineering response

The prototype combines buoyancy, propulsion, ultrasonic sensing, microcontroller programming, and an integrated collection net.

03

Research focus

Performance is evaluated through waste collection efficiency, obstacle avoidance, navigation performance, battery operating time, and technical performance indicators.

Research status: Experimental values such as collection capacity, operating time, and maximum load-bearing capacity are still to be measured. This website does not present fabricated results.

03 · Technology

Inside the system.

T.I.D.E. BOT is built around an Arduino Uno R3, dual ultrasonic sensors, a dual H-bridge motor driver, two propeller-driven motors, a floating hull, and an integrated collection net.

System architecture

Sense

Two HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors provide distance readings for obstacle detection.

Process

The Arduino Uno receives sensor information and applies the programmed navigation logic.

Actuate

The L298N motor driver controls two DC motors and their propellers for movement and steering.

Collect

An integrated nylon mesh collection net at the front is intended to retain floating solid waste.

04 · Methodology

How the prototype is developed and evaluated.

The study uses an evaluation/usability testing design under an experimental category within a research-and-development product development framework.

01

Needs assessment & concept

Observe shoreline waters in Pachoca and assess the site's solid-waste pollution and testing conditions.

02

Prototype construction

Assemble the electronics inside a waterproof enclosure, integrate buoyant structural materials, and connect the propulsion system.

03

Pre-testing & optimization

Calibrate and check the prototype before formal solid-waste collection trials.

04

Expert evaluation

Three subject-matter experts evaluate structural stability, mechanical safety, material viability, and operational safety before trials.

05

Field testing

Use standardized target sampling of selected floating solid waste such as bottles, sachets, food packaging, and wrappers.

06

Data analysis

Record and organize performance observations, then use mean and standard deviation to summarize the experimental data.

Sampling: The paper describes non-human/environmental sampling using standardized target sampling, with consistent test area, starting conditions, procedure, and observations of weather, wind, and waves.
05 · Performance Evaluation

What will be measured?

The revised paper identifies the following performance areas. Actual numerical results are intentionally left open until experimental testing is completed.

Waste collection efficiency

How effectively the collection system gathers the selected floating solid waste.

Obstacle avoidance

How accurately the dual ultrasonic sensing system supports obstacle detection and response.

Navigation performance

How the vehicle moves and maneuvers during testing under the observed environmental conditions.

Battery operating time

Measured duration of operation during experimental testing.

Load-bearing capacity

The maximum experimentally measured load the floating structure can support.

Waterproofing

Protection of electronics from water exposure during testing and resistance of structural materials to the test environment.

06 · Scope & Safety

Designed for a defined test environment.

Research locale

Ocean/shoreline waters of Barangay Pachoca, Calapan City, Oriental Mindoro.

Included waste

Floating solid waste within the intended collection capacity, including bottles, sachets, food packaging, and wrappers.

Not covered

Microplastics, non-solid waste, water-pollution levels, and testing in rivers or lakes are outside the stated scope.

Environmental care

Testing should minimize disturbance to aquatic life. Accidentally collected organisms are to be released, and collected waste properly disposed of.

Field limitations

Weather, waves, wind, limited time, and budget may affect testing conditions and repeatability.

Operational safety

The prototype, battery, electrical system, motors, and testing area must be checked before operation, with testing stopped under unsafe conditions.
